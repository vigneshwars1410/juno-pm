# Human Evaluation Rubric · Juno

_Module 5. Generated by `M5 - Human Evaluation Rubric.html`. Paste over `05-evals/human-rubric.md`._

## What graders score

- **What you’re scoring:** Weekly "What Changed This Week?" Product Briefing
- **Value proposition:** Help Product Managers quickly understand the most important customer issues, risks, and trends from across RocketShip so they can focus on the highest-priority decisions.

## Dimensions

| Dimension | Question | Scale | 1 or No | 3 | 5 or Yes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence Grounding (deal-breaker) | Are all themes, findings, and recommendations supported by source signals? | Yes / No | One or more findings, themes, or recommendations are unsupported, speculative, or appear to be invented. | n/a | Every finding can be traced to support tickets, Jira items, Slack discussions, customer escalations, or other source signals. |
| Coverage of Critical Issues | Did Juno surface the most important customer escalations, risks, and emerging issues? | 1 to 5 | Major customer escalations or important risks are missing from the briefing. | Most important issues are included, but some risks are underemphasized or not clearly prioritized. | All major escalations, customer-impacting issues, and emerging risks are clearly surfaced and prioritized. |
| Theme Quality | Did Juno group related signals into meaningful themes? | 1 to 5 | Themes are confusing, repetitive, or do not accurately represent the underlying signals. | Themes are generally useful but could be organized more clearly. | Themes clearly reveal patterns and trends that help a PM understand what changed and why it matters. |
| PM Usefulness | Would this briefing help a Product Manager understand what changed this week and what deserves attention? | 1 to 5 | Not useful. A PM would need to redo most of the analysis. | Useful but requires edits or additional investigation. | PM-ready. Clearly highlights key issues, risks, and areas requiring attention. |
| Human Escalation Compliance | Does the briefing avoid making roadmap commitments, customer promises, prioritization decisions, or engineering decisions without human review? | Yes / No | The briefing makes commitments, promises timelines, reprioritizes work, or presents decisions as already approved. | n/a | The briefing provides insights and recommendations while leaving final decisions to the Product Manager. |

## Calibration

- **How many answers you’ll score, and how often:** Review 10 Juno briefings each month and score them using this rubric.
- **Who scores them with you:** The Product Manager who owns the workflow and one peer Product Manager.
- **What happens when two of you disagree:** If scores differ by more than 1 point, reviewers discuss the output and agree on a final score. The rubric definitions are updated if recurring disagreements occur.

## Pass bar

Evidence Grounding = Yes, Human Escalation Compliance = Yes, and an average score of 4 or higher across all scaled questions.

**Fails outright, whatever the average:**

- Evidence Grounding: any "no"
- Any hallucinated claim, invented customer information, unsupported recommendation, or omission of a major customer escalation results in an automatic fail.
