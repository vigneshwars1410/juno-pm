## Problem & user

RocketShip PMs need this handled: Juno PM is an AI Associate Product Manager for RocketShip. For this prototype, Juno's job is to solve "Signal Collapse" by synthesizing signals from across the company and producing a concise"What Changed This Week?" briefing for Product Managers. Instead of making decisions, Juno helps PMs understand what needs attention so they can make faster, better-informed decisions.. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

## 01 Context · Data Requirements

**Required sources, and exclusions**

Juno can see:
- Support tickets and customer escalations
- Jira bugs and feature requests
- Slack discussions
- Sales notes and blocked deals
- Product roadmap status
- Engineering capacity constraints
- Company strategy priorities

Juno uses this context to determine which issues are emerging, which customers are affected, and which signals deserve attention in the weekly briefing. The project brief describes Juno as operating inside Slack, Notion, and Jira to synthesize scattered signals into insight.

**How fresh must it be**

Real-time triggers:
Jira issues, support escalations, and Slack messages are indexed within 5 minutes of creation or update. Product briefs, risk summaries, and prioritization recommendations always use the latest available data at query time.

Refresh cadence:
Notion documentation, roadmap status, and engineering capacity data are refreshed every 30 minutes. Weekly briefing reports are regenerated from current sources rather than cached summaries.

Unreachable sources:
If Jira, Slack, or Notion cannot be reached, Juno does not generate recommendations that depend on the missing source. Instead, it identifies the unavailable system, lists the affected analysis, and routes the request to a human PM for review.

Staleness threshold:
If any source is older than 24 hours, Juno marks the output as "Data May Be Outdated" and cites the timestamp of the last successful refresh.

Fail-safe behavior:
Juno may summarize available evidence but cannot recommend roadmap changes, customer commitments, or priority shifts when a required source is unavailable.

## 02 Tools · System Capabilities

**The verb list**

Juno can:
- Aggregate signals from multiple sources
- Group related signals into themes
- Identify trends and recurring issues
- Generate a weekly summary
- Highlight risks and customer impact
- Recommend areas for PM review
- Link findings back to source evidence

Juno cannot:
- Commit roadmap changes
- Promise delivery dates
- Reprioritize work automatically
- Make customer commitments
- Override PM decisions

Juno is an assistant that supports judgment rather than replacing it.

**Deliberate omissions**

No auto_reprioritize_roadmap() tool.

Juno may recommend priority changes based on evidence from Slack, Jira, and Notion, but it cannot directly modify roadmap priorities. Roadmap decisions involve trade-offs between customer impact, business strategy, engineering capacity, and executive priorities that require human judgment. This ensures Juno supports PM decision-making rather than replacing it.

## 03 Loop · AI Costs & Latency

**Turn ceiling and escalation**

2 attempts
- Analyze incoming signals and generate themes.
- Re-cluster or refine results if confidence is low or signals conflict.

After that, Juno presents the summary to the PM rather than continuing autonomously.

**Latency and cost target**

Response target:
Weekly signal summaries and risk assessments are generated in under 10 seconds for 95% of requests.

Turn limit:
Maximum 2 analysis passes per request. Additional investigation requires PM review.

Cost target:
Average processing cost remains below $0.10 per analysis request and below $20 per PM per month.

Escalation trigger:
Juno stops and escalates when:
- Confidence falls below 80%
- Sources contain conflicting evidence
- Required data is unavailable
- The recommendation would affect roadmap priority or customer commitments

Juno returns its findings and supporting evidence rather than continuing autonomous analysis.

## 04 Memory · Data Requirements

**What persists, at what scope**

Juno remembers:
- Previously identified themes
- Open risks
- Major escalations
- Historical trends
- Past weekly briefs

This allows Juno to recognize whether an issue is new, recurring, improving, or getting worse over time.

**Expiry and write rules**

Retention period:
Themes, risk patterns, escalations, and weekly briefs are retained for 12 months to support trend analysis and recurring issue detection.

Expiration:
Resolved risks and closed escalations are archived after 90 days and excluded from active prioritization unless referenced again.

Write rules:
Juno may update its stored themes and risk clusters when new evidence is found in Slack, Jira, or Notion.

Human override:
Any PM correction, roadmap decision, or manually assigned priority immediately overrides Juno's stored assessment. Human decisions are preserved as the source of truth and cannot be overwritten by future AI analysis.

Cross-user scope:
Team-level themes and risk trends may be shared across PMs. Personal notes, drafts, and user-specific interactions are not shared between users.

## 05 Permissions · AI Risks & Mitigations

**The permission tier**

| Side-effect class | Tier |
|---|---|
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

**Justification**

Juno may automatically:
- Read and synthesize approved data sources
- Generate weekly briefings
- Highlight emerging risks
- Group signals into themes
- Surface supporting evidence
- Notify a PM when a significant trend emerges

Juno must ask for human approval before:
- Changing priorities
- Recommending roadmap commitments
- Communicating with customers
- Escalating severity levels
- Making resource allocation decisions

Because Juno influences roadmap, customer, and engineering discussions, final judgment remains with the human Product Manager.

## 06 Verification · AI Testing & Measurement

**The check before output ships**

Juno checks:
- Every reported theme is supported by one or more underlying signals.
- Escalations and high-impact customer issues are included in the summary.
- Recommendations are linked to source evidence.
- No themes are invented without supporting data.
- The generated briefing aligns with RocketShip's goal of helping PMs answer questions faster and reduce Signal Collapse.

Verification output:
- Confidence: High / Medium / Low
- Number of supporting signals per theme
- Links to source tickets, Slack discussions, or escalations
- Any gaps or missing context requiring PM review

Human validation: A Product Manager reviews the briefing and confirms:
"Does this accurately represent the most important things that changed this week?"

If yes, Juno's output is considered successful. Formal metrics and evaluation criteria will be defined later in the project when impact measurement is introduced.

**Failure behaviour**

If verification fails, Juno does not make a recommendation.

Instead, Juno returns:
- The evidence it was able to find
- The missing or conflicting information
- A low-confidence warning
- A request for PM review

If fewer than 3 supporting signals exist, or sources conflict significantly, Juno labels the finding as "Unverified" and excludes it from roadmap prioritization recommendations.

Juno never invents themes, customer impact, or roadmap priorities to fill evidence gaps.
