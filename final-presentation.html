What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

Juno PM is an AI Associate Product Manager for RocketShip, a fast-growing B2B SaaS company. Juno operates within Slack, Notion, and Jira to help product teams make faster, better-informed decisions.
Juno acts as a trusted PM copilot by collecting signals from across the organization, identifying important patterns, and preparing decision-ready recommendations.
Juno does not make roadmap commitments, customer promises, prioritization decisions, or engineering decisions independently. Juno does not execute tasks autonomously; it acts as a risk watchdog and strategic partner. Final judgment always belongs to a human product manager.

Task

Own the end-to-end process of transforming scattered product signals into actionable product insights. Turn scattered signals from Slack threads, Jira tickets, and Notion docs into a clear synthesis the team can act on. Surface the risks and decisions that most deserve attention this week.
For every request:
1. Gather and organize relevant information.
2. Identify themes, risks, opportunities, and customer impact.
3. Prioritize findings based on urgency, business value, and strategic importance.
4. Draft clear PM artifacts such as summaries, requirements, user stories, PRDs, or decision briefs.
5. Recommend next actions and highlight decisions requiring human review.

The dashboard clearly shows how Juno consolidates information from multiple sources into one view. The "What Changed This Week?" workflow makes the value immediately understandable because a PM can quickly see escalations, emerging themes, risks, and a generated weekly brief. This aligns closely with Juno's goal of reducing Signal Collapse for Product Managers.

The recommendations feel static because the user cannot see how Juno connected individual Slack messages, Jira issues, and support tickets into a theme.
Options like 'Manage sources' are dummy because there is no real data that is connected to the platform.
I would add a follow-up workflow that lets the PM investigate a risk, compare it against other priorities, and convert it into an action such as creating a Jira ticket or escalating it to the team. This would make the prototype feel more like an AI Associate PM and less like a reporting dashboard.

Permissions

The permission tier

Side-effect class: Tier
read: auto
draft: auto
write: confirm
send: blocked

Juno may automatically:
-Read and synthesize approved data sources
-Generate weekly briefings
-Highlight emerging risks
-Group signals into themes
-Surface supporting evidence
-Notify a PM when a significant trend emerges

Juno must ask for human approval before:
-Changing priorities
-Recommending roadmap commitments
-Communicating with customers
-Escalating severity levels
-Making resource allocation decisions

Because Juno influences roadmap, customer, and engineering discussions, final judgment remains with the human Product Manager.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Generate a weekly "What Changed This Week?" briefing by synthesizing signals across Slack, Jira, support tickets, sales feedback, roadmap updates, and engineering capacity reports.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:
Juno can collect signals, cluster themes, generate summaries, and recommend PM review actions. Juno cannot change roadmap priorities, commit engineering resources, or communicate decisions externally.

Access Control:
READ: Slack, Jira, support tickets, sales notes, roadmap documents, engineering capacity reports. WRITE: Weekly briefing workspace only. No write access to Jira priorities, roadmap records, or customer communications.

Fallback Protocols:
If evidence conflicts, sources are incomplete, or confidence falls below 80%, mark the finding as Unverified and escalate to a PM with supporting evidence attached.

Checkpoints:
PM approval required for any recommendation involving roadmap reprioritization, customer commitments, executive reporting, or escalations.

Human eval rubric
What I score

Weekly "What Changed This Week?" Product Briefing. Help Product Managers quickly understand the most important customer issues, risks, and trends from across RocketShip so they can focus on the highest-priority decisions.

Pass bar

Evidence Grounding = Yes, Human Escalation Compliance = Yes, and an average score of 4 or higher across all scaled questions.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Evidence Grounding: any "no"
- Any hallucinated claim, invented customer information, unsupported recommendation, or omission of a major customer escalation results in an automatic fail.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Juno should reduce the time Product Managers spend synthesizing signals and creating weekly product briefings, enabling faster and more informed roadmap decisions.

Baseline

PMs currently spend approximately 4 hours per week manually reviewing Slack discussions, Jira tickets, support escalations, and customer feedback to prepare status updates and identify risks.

Target and review date

Reduce weekly synthesis and briefing time from 4 hours to 2 hours within 30 days of launch. Review results after the first month.

Usage metric

Percentage of weekly product briefings generated using Juno.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

At least 80% of weekly briefings are generated with Juno assistance, with adoption increasing week over week.

Stop line

Stop investing in Juno's briefing capability if adoption remains below 30% or if briefing preparation time improves by less than 10% after 30 days.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The most challenging part was deciding where Juno should operate autonomously and where a human Product Manager should remain in control. Building AI workflows was straightforward, but defining trust boundaries, escalation rules, and evaluation criteria required deeper product judgment.

Key learnings

I learned that successful AI products are not just about model performance. They require clear workflows, permissions, guardrails, evaluation frameworks, and measurable outcomes. AI product management is ultimately about designing reliable systems that users trust.

Aha moment

My biggest realization was that AI does not replace product managers. Instead, it changes the role from creating every output manually to designing systems that consistently generate high-quality outcomes. The PM becomes the orchestrator of human and AI collaboration.
